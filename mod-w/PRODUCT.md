# PRODUCT.md — Frank McGuire Portfolio

**Product:** Professional portfolio website for Frank McGuire  
**Target URL:** https://www.frank-mcguire.com/  
**Status:** Approved v1.0 for MOD-W implementation and publication baseline  
**Product Owner:** ChatGPT acting as Product Owner in MOD-W Product Definition mode  
**Moderator:** Frank McGuire  
**Primary downstream use:** Claude Design prototyping, then MOD-W development validation

---

## 1. Product summary

Replace the current minimal Frontend Developer website with a professional compact single-page application (SPA) portfolio that positions Frank McGuire as:

1. a senior frontend engineer available for full-time roles,
2. a senior frontend consultant available for freelance contracts, and
3. the author and practitioner of MOD-W, a disciplined human-in-the-loop workflow for AI-assisted software development.

The portfolio must attract both full-time employment and freelance contract opportunities with equal priority. It must also serve as a public validation surface for MOD-W by showing that Frank uses the workflow to produce real, reviewable, testable software projects.

The website should be credible to recruiters, hiring managers, engineering leaders, and potential consulting clients. It should feel like a senior engineer's portfolio, not a novelty AI showcase.

---

## 2. Problem statement

The current website is too minimal to support Frank's current positioning. It lists identity, stack, project links, MOD-W authorship, and contact links, but it does not sufficiently explain:

- why Frank is a strong senior frontend hire,
- what kinds of full-time or freelance opportunities he is targeting,
- what business and technical outcomes he has delivered,
- how recent projects validate modern Angular, real-time dashboarding, testing, and architecture capability,
- what MOD-W is and why it matters,
- how visitors should evaluate Frank and take the next step.

The new portfolio must convert passive interest into qualified conversations.

## 4. Target audiences

### 4.1 Full-time recruiters

Recruiters need to quickly understand:

- Frank is a Senior Frontend Engineer based in Germany.
- He is available for full-time remote or hybrid roles in Germany / EU.
- Angular and TypeScript are primary strengths.
- He also has credible Vue, Nuxt, and React experience.
- He has relevant domain experience in B2B SaaS, logistics, travel, e-commerce, and real-time dashboards.
- He has work authorization and contact information.

Primary recruiter success outcome: recruiter contacts Frank about a relevant senior frontend role.

### 4.2 Hiring managers and engineering leads

Hiring managers need to evaluate depth, judgment, and relevance:

- Can Frank own frontend architecture and implementation?
- Has he modernized legacy systems?
- Can he work in complex domains?
- Does he understand testing, CI, maintainability, and performance?
- Can he mentor and raise engineering quality?
- Is MOD-W evidence of discipline or just AI branding?

Primary hiring-manager success outcome: manager wants to schedule a technical conversation.

### 4.3 Freelance clients and contract recruiters

Freelance visitors need to quickly determine:

- Frank is available for B2B freelance contracts.
- He can deliver independently or embed with an existing team.
- He has current independent product-building experience through Cavalieri Align / MQTT-Align.
- He can support greenfield frontend work, modernization, dashboarding, and Angular-heavy delivery.
- He can communicate clearly and work through structured milestones.

Primary freelance success outcome: visitor contacts Frank with a concrete project or contract opportunity.

### 4.4 MOD-W evaluators

MOD-W visitors may be engineering leaders, senior developers, AI-tooling skeptics, or potential consulting clients. They need to understand:

- MOD-W is a human-in-the-loop governance methodology for AI-assisted software development.
- It separates product, technical, implementation, QA, and Moderator responsibilities.
- It uses documents, acceptance checks, review gates, and cross-validation to reduce AI drift.
- Frank uses it in his own Angular / SaaS projects.
- It is relevant to teams that want AI-assisted development without sacrificing quality.

Primary MOD-W success outcome: visitor reads enough to trust the methodology and explores the repository or contacts Frank about setup, training, or adoption.

### 8.3 MOD-W section

The MOD-W section is a major pillar and must be understandable to non-specialists and credible to engineers. It should be concise on the main page, with outbound links to the full MOD-W repository for deeper reading instead of duplicating long documentation.

Required content:

- Plain-language definition of MOD-W.
- Statement that MOD-W is not an automation framework or autonomous agent framework.
- Explanation of the problem MOD-W solves: opaque AI code dumps, lost context, weak validation, blurred responsibility.
- Explanation of the core idea: role separation, documents, review gates, cross-validation, human Moderator approval.
- Short role model: Moderator, Product Owner, Designer + Prototyper, Tech Lead, Development Team, QA / Tester.
- Practical evidence: projects built with MOD-W.
- Link to GitHub repository.
- CTA for MOD-W consulting, training, or adoption discussion.

Tone requirement:

- Confident but not inflated.
- Practical, not manifesto-heavy.
- Avoid implying MOD-W guarantees quality by itself.
- Emphasize that human judgment and engineering discipline remain central.

The About section must provide a concise career narrative that frames Frank's long career as continuous adaptation.

Required themes:

Required content:

## 11. Non-functional requirements

### NFR1 — Professional tone

The website must feel credible for senior engineering hiring and B2B consulting. It must avoid gimmicky AI branding.

### NFR1a — Minimalist modern design

The website must use a minimalist, modern design direction with restrained visuals, clear typography, generous spacing, and low visual clutter. It must not feel flashy, gimmicky, overly animated, or visually dense.

### NFR2 — Accessibility

The site must be designed and implemented with accessibility in mind. Text, navigation, contrast, keyboard interaction, and responsive behavior should support inclusive use.

### NFR3 — Performance

The site should load quickly and avoid unnecessary visual or JavaScript weight. Portfolio polish must not come at the expense of basic performance.

### NFR4 — Mobile usability

The site must work well on mobile, tablet, and desktop. Recruiters may review it from mobile devices.

### NFR5 — Maintainability

The site content structure should be maintainable. Projects, case studies, CTAs, and availability language should be easy to update.

### NFR6 — Trustworthiness

The site must not exaggerate, fabricate, or blur distinctions between employment work, proprietary work, public demos, and methodology claims.

### NFR7 — MOD-W validation compatibility

The site must be suitable for MOD-W development validation. Requirements must be testable through acceptance checks, content review, and manual inspection.
